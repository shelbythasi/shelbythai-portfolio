ACADEMIC PROJECT · 01

Business Intelligence & Sales Performance

Business question

Which products, suppliers, and regions drive business performance—and which deserve a closer look? Transactional data becomes more useful when decision-makers can compare revenue, cost, and net revenue in one reporting model.

Analytical approach

Connect the data. Linked orders to customer, product, supplier, and country attributes to support consistent analysis.

Define the measures. Calculated Gross Revenue as Quantity × List Price, Cost as Quantity × Unit Cost, and Net Revenue as Gross Revenue − Cost.

Build interactive views. Used filters, drill-downs, parameters, LOD calculations, maps, and time-based views to explore financial performance.

Support decisions. Made product, supplier, and regional contributions comparable for performance reviews.

Decision value

  • A shared reporting model makes revenue, cost, and net revenue easier to compare across business dimensions.
  • Parameter-driven measures and drill-downs let users investigate regional, supplier, and product performance without rebuilding a report.
  • Product comparisons and time-based views provide a starting point for investigating underperformance.

This project demonstrates reporting and analysis capabilities. It does not claim a measured increase in revenue or a production deployment.

Smart Retail Analytics Hub

Business question

Retail teams need reliable operational data and a way to analyze historical performance. Which products need replenishment, who are the highest-value customers, and which items are frequently purchased together?

Analytical approach

Model operations. Organized normalized transactional tables to represent customers, products, stores, orders, and inventory.

Structure analytical reporting. Used a sales star schema to support aggregation across business dimensions.

Write decision-focused queries. Analyzed average order value, store and category performance, low stock, and high-value customers.

Explore personalization. Used JSON preferences and loyalty attributes for category-based recommendations and pricing logic.

Decision value

  • Inventory classifications identify products that warrant a replenishment review.
  • Frequently bought-together analysis highlights candidates for bundles and cross-selling.
  • Customer and loyalty attributes can support more targeted recommendations.

The project demonstrates analytical use cases on academic project data. Future dashboards, forecasting, and AI integrations are design possibilities, not completed production features.

Global Innovation & Economic Inclusion

Business question

Economic strength does not tell the whole story of a country's innovation or workforce inclusion. How do R&D investment, gender inequality, participation, and leadership representation relate across countries?

Analytical approach

Integrate indicators. Combined economic, innovation, and inclusion measures from multiple institutions.

Prepare comparable views. Used Tableau Prep to clean and organize the data for cross-country exploration.

Build linked dashboards. Compared economic strength, innovation investment, efficiency, and workforce inclusion.

Interpret relationships. Summarized observed associations while accounting for country-specific structural differences.

Decision value

  • Higher economic strength did not always correspond to greater R&D investment.
  • Countries with lower gender inequality generally showed higher innovation investment in the analysis.
  • Female inclusion differed across labor participation, leadership representation, and legal frameworks.

These are observed cross-country associations, not evidence that one variable causes another. Country context and differences in measurement remain important.
